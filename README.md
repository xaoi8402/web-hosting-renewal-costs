## The renewal price is the real price

For most mainstream hosts, the advertised rate is a coupon tied to a long prepay. The renewal price is routinely two to six times higher, and almost nobody leads with it. IONOS is the extreme case: $1/mo for the first term, roughly $10/mo after — a 900% jump. SiteGround's $2.99 becomes $17.99, a 502% increase that makes it the steepest jump among well-known hosts.

A big markup isn't automatically a scam — support staff and infrastructure cost real money, and acquisition-priced hosting subsidizes your first term. But it does mean every comparison that ranks hosts by entry price is ranking them by the number they want you to see. Only Kinsta and Cloudways charge the same price forever (Cloudways even bills hourly, with a 3-day trial instead of a money-back window), and InterServer comes close: $2.50 buys your first month, then it's $7/mo indefinitely.

## Fine print that changes the math

The specs that decide whether a plan works usually live in the terms, not the pricing table:

- Hostinger's shared plans cap inodes at 250,000 and throttle write speeds heavily; there's no managed VPS option either, and support is chat-only.
- SiteGround's GrowBig applies a 1 GB fair-use cap per database — fine for most sites, a problem for bloated WordPress installs.
- DreamHost's old "unlimited everything" reputation is outdated: its tiers carry fixed 25/50/100 GB NVMe caps.
- Bluehost's checkout leans hard on add-on upsells, and the platform is mid-way through migrating over a million customers to Oracle Cloud.
- Liquid Web's cheapest managed-WP plan skips firewall protection and includes no free domain — though it backs a 99.99% uptime guarantee and never raises renewal prices.
- GreenGeeks requires a three-year commitment for its intro price, and phone support isn't 24/7.

Worth noting where the industry is heading: the mainstream hosts now bundle AI builders (Hostinger's Horizons generates apps from a text prompt, DreamHost relaunched its Remixer AI builder, Bluehost re-platformed onto Oracle Cloud). None of it changes the renewal math. RackNerd does none of it — it sells cheap, boring infrastructure. Depending on your project, one of those two approaches is exactly what you want.

## FAQ

**Is $2.99/mo hosting really $2.99/mo?**
No. That rate almost always requires a 36–48 month prepay and renews at two to six times the headline: Hostinger's $2.99 renews at $10.99, SiteGround's at $17.99, and IONOS's $1 teaser at around $10.

**Why doesn't RackNerd show up in most web hosting comparison articles?**
Because mainstream shortlists are largely built around hosts with big affiliate programs and long feature checklists. RackNerd distributes through community deals and word of mouth, so it stays off those lists. Its absence reflects distribution, not quality — but it does mean fewer conveniences and fewer tutorials when something breaks.

**Can a $10–25/year host actually run a real website?**
Yes, if traffic is modest. A cPanel shared plan with SSD storage and unmetered bandwidth handles a blog, portfolio, or small business brochure site without complaint. You'll manage more yourself and rely on ticket support. If the site directly earns money, the calculus shifts — at that point the difference between $15/year and $130/year is cheap insurance.
